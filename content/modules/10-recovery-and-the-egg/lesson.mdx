## Slow the landing, cushion the egg

A **<GlossaryTerm id="parachute">parachute</GlossaryTerm>** increases drag. A larger working canopy usually means slower descent and a softer landing, but more flight time and more chance to drift away.

Padding spreads the stopping force. Think of catching a ball while moving your hands backward instead of holding them rigid.

## Protect both eggs

Cushion every side, including between the eggs. Keep hard electronics and fasteners away from the shells. Hold the eggs securely without squeezing them. Make the bay easy to open for inspection.

## Make recovery repeatable

- Keep all rocket sections connected with secure recovery attachments.
- Protect the chute from ejection heat with suitable flame-resistant wadding or a recovery blanket, following its instructions. Ordinary tissue is not a substitute.
- Pack the chute and lines consistently. Check that the bundle slides out freely instead of wedging in the tube.
- Inspect for scorched fabric, tangled lines, or worn attachments after every flight.

## Change one thing

If a safe flight lasts too long, investigate recovery size and flight conditions. If the egg cracks, inspect padding, contact points, deployment, and landing speed before trying again. Do not trade away safe recovery just to improve time.
