## Push strength and total push

**Thrust** is the force pushing the rocket, measured in newtons (N). **Total impulse** adds up that thrust over the burn, measured in newton-seconds (N·s).

Think of pushing a skateboard: a strong quick push and a gentler long push can give similar total pushes. They do not accelerate it the same way.

## Read a motor code

Example: **C6-5**.

- **C:** total-impulse class, not an exact altitude.
- **6:** nominal average thrust in newtons, not burn time.
- **5:** delay in seconds between burnout and ejection.

This example teaches the code. It is not a motor recommendation for your rocket. A **thrust curve** plots push against time; the area underneath is total impulse. Two motors in the same letter class can have different thrust curves.

## Burn, coast, recover

1. **Burn:** exhaust goes backward; thrust pushes the rocket forward.
2. **Coast:** after burnout, the rocket still climbs while slowing down.
3. **Eject:** the motor's ejection charge releases the recovery system near <GlossaryTerm id="apogee">apogee</GlossaryTerm>.
