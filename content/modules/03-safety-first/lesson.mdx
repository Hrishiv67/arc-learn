## Know when to stop

Fly with your adult adviser and an experienced launch crew. Read the **NAR Model Rocket Safety Code** in the handbook together before handling motors.

## The checks that matter

- Use lightweight, nonmetal nose cones, bodies, and fins. Use certified commercial motors exactly as instructed; never make fuel or modify a motor.
- Use an approved open launch site with permission. The launch crew checks field size, wind, airspace, and fire conditions. Do not launch near aircraft, into clouds, or over dry grass that could burn.
- Follow the crew's spectator distances and countdown. Electrical launch equipment must have its safety interlock and return-to-off switch.
- Stop for a damaged fin, loose motor retainer, jammed recovery system, or unexpected simulation warning. Have the problem checked before flight.

## If the motor does not start

Stay back. The launch operator removes the safety interlock or disconnects power, then waits **at least 60 seconds** after the last launch attempt before anyone approaches. Follow the range officer's instructions.

## Recovery is part of safety

Never try to catch a descending rocket. Never climb a power line or tree or enter a road to retrieve one. Point it out to the adult launch crew.
