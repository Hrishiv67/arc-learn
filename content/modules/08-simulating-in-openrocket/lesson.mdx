## Make a digital copy

**<GlossaryTerm id="openrocket">OpenRocket</GlossaryTerm>** predicts a flight before you spend time building. Start with an example model to learn the controls, then create your team's own design.

## Your first simulation

1. **Rocket design:** add the nose, tubes, fins, motor mount, payload, and recovery gear. Enter measured sizes and masses.
2. **Motors & Configuration:** choose the motor mount, exact motor, and available ejection delay. Check the ARC-approved list separately.
3. **Flight simulations:** enter the launch guide and weather conditions. Run the simulation.
4. **Read the result:** inspect peak altitude, speed off the guide, deployment speed, landing speed, and warnings—not just height.

The [official illustrated walkthrough](https://openrocket.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user_guide/basic_flight_simulation.html) shows the actual controls.

## Fix warnings first

Too little speed leaving the guide can mean an unstable launch. Early or late recovery can open the chute at damaging speed. A predicted target altitude does not cancel those problems.

## Make it match reality

After building, compare the actual mass and balance point with your model. Include glue and paint. Keep a saved baseline and change one setting at a time. Practice flights test the prediction.
