## Two points to know

The **<GlossaryTerm id="center-of-gravity">center of gravity (CG)</GlossaryTerm>** is the balance point. The **<GlossaryTerm id="center-of-pressure">center of pressure (CP)</GlossaryTerm>** is where the sideways air force effectively acts.

For a stable model rocket, **CG must be closer to the nose than CP**. If a gust tips it, the air pushes the tail back into line. Think of the feathers at the back of an arrow.

## Check the rocket you will actually fly

Include the motor, eggs, parachute, electronics, and paint. A heavier motor moves CG toward the tail; added nose mass moves it forward. Changing fin size changes CP.

The **<GlossaryTerm id="stability-margin">stability margin</GlossaryTerm>** is the CG–CP gap, often divided by body diameter and shown in _calibers_. One caliber means one body diameter. Ask your mentor to review the margin and launch speed; a positive margin alone does not guarantee a safe flight.

## Fix the cause

Straight, firmly attached fins matter. Larger fins can improve stability but add drag. Nose ballast can help but adds weight. Simulate the change and recheck the finished rocket; more ballast is not automatically better.

<Callout title="Quick check" tone="info">
  Point to CG and CP on your simulated rocket. Which one is nearer the nose?
  Then check that the simulation uses your real loaded mass.
</Callout>
