## Inner systems (what flies the mission)

### 1. Motors — climb, coast, then pop

A model rocket <GlossaryTerm id="motor">motor</GlossaryTerm> burns in three phases:

1. **Thrust** — hot gas out the nozzle pushes the rocket up  
2. **Delay** — smoke only; the rocket coasts toward <GlossaryTerm id="apogee">apogee</GlossaryTerm>  
3. **Ejection** — a gas pulse opens the recovery bay

Motor codes: letter = total impulse class · middle number = average thrust (N) · last number = delay seconds. Example: **F42-6**.

<MotorPhases className="w-full my-6" />

**ARC rule:** only **class F** motors from the approved list. Different F motors still climb differently — compare thrust curves before you pick one.

### 2. Altimeter — how altitude is scored

The <GlossaryTerm id="altimeter">altimeter</GlossaryTerm> reads air pressure to get altitude (and often more flight data). **ARC allows only these four:**

- PerfectFlite Pnut  
- PerfectFlite FireFly  
- Jolly Logic Altimeter One  
- Jolly Logic Altimeter Two  

Protect it in a hard case. Give it **vent holes** to outside air — a sealed bay gives bad numbers.

### 3. Parachute + Nomex — come down without burning up

After ejection, the <GlossaryTerm id="parachute">parachute</GlossaryTerm> slows the rocket. **ARC requires cotton canopies.** Bigger chute = slower fall = longer flight time. A spill hole can shorten time if you are overshooting the window — it is a risk trade, not a free fix.

<LessonPhoto
  src="/images/parts/parachute-flight-clean.jpg"
  alt="Competition rocket descending under an orange parachute after a successful flight"
  caption="Recovery in the field. Size and packing decide whether you land inside the duration window with an intact egg."
/>

Fold cleanly, then protect the pack from ejection heat with <GlossaryTerm id="nomex">Nomex</GlossaryTerm> (real fire-resistant fabric — not tissue or regular cloth).

  Nomex goes between the ejection charge and the chute/shock cord. Keep the wrap
  loose enough that the parachute can still open instantly.
</Callout>

### 4. Egg protection case — survive the landing

The egg must stay uncracked. Pad it on **all sides**, keep mass in a fixed place for a predictable <GlossaryTerm id="center-of-gravity">CG</GlossaryTerm>, and build a case you can open for pre- and post-flight inspection.

## Outer airframe (what you see on the pad)

### Body tube, nose cone, fins

| Part | Job | ARC / safety notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <GlossaryTerm id="body-tube">Body tube</GlossaryTerm> | Structure + houses everything | Same diameter nose→tail · ≥ **47 mm (1.85 in)** · overall length ≥ **650 mm** · lightweight non-metal |
| <GlossaryTerm id="nose-cone">Nose cone</GlossaryTerm> | Cuts drag; often holds shock cord | Lightweight non-metal · must separate cleanly at ejection · do pull tests on the bench |

## Before you cut: simulate, then prove it

Use <GlossaryTerm id="openrocket">OpenRocket</GlossaryTerm> (free) to model tube, nose, fins, motor, chute, and egg mass. Check predicted altitude, flight time, stability margin, and descent rate **before** you glue.

Then fly. Compare the altimeter to the sim and adjust the model (often drag) so the next prediction matches reality.

<Callout tone="caution" title="Sim ≠ score">
  OpenRocket narrows options fast. Practice flights decide what is true on your
  build, in your wind, with your finish and mass.
</Callout>

## Quick fault map (use after every flight)

| If you see… | Check first |
| --- | --- |
| Wrong height / weird timing of chute | Motor delay · ejection · packing |
| Nonsense altitude number | Altimeter vents · case · battery |
| Burned chute / failed deploy | Nomex placement · packing tightness |
| Cracked egg | Egg padding · descent rate · landing attitude |
| Crooked flight / broken fin | Fins · CG/CP · nose fit / separation |

You do not need every brand memorized. You need this map — and the photos above — so you can build, talk, and debug like a team that knows its rocket.
