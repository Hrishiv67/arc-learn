## The flight goal for this season

Carry the eggs to the target height and bring them back uncracked, within the time window.

<SeasonFigures />

**<GlossaryTerm id="apogee">Apogee</GlossaryTerm>** is the highest point—not where the rocket lands. An onboard **<GlossaryTerm id="altimeter">altimeter</GlossaryTerm>** measures it. **<GlossaryTerm id="duration">Duration</GlossaryTerm>** starts at first motion and ends when the first piece touches ground or a tree, or is lost from view. Official timers measure it.

**<GlossaryTerm id="liftoff-mass">Liftoff mass</GlossaryTerm>** includes everything: motor, eggs, recovery gear, electronics, glue, and paint. Weigh the complete rocket.

## The parts of your rocket

<SourceFigure name="parts" />

- **Nose cone, body tube, fins:** shape, structure, and stability.
- **Motor mount:** holds the motor straight and transfers its push into the rocket.
- **Payload bay:** protects the eggs; a secured, vented bay holds the altimeter.
- **Recovery system:** parachute slows descent; shock cord keeps the sections connected.

## Why a rocket flies straight

The **<GlossaryTerm id="center-of-gravity">CG</GlossaryTerm>** is the balance point. The **<GlossaryTerm id="center-of-pressure">CP</GlossaryTerm>** is where the air force effectively acts. For stability, CG belongs nearer the nose than CP. The gap is the **<GlossaryTerm id="stability-margin">stability margin</GlossaryTerm>**. Check it with the rocket fully loaded.

## How a flight is scored

**Lower is better.** Altitude points equal the distance in feet from the target, above or below. Duration points are zero inside the window; outside it, multiply the seconds from the nearest edge by **{SEASON.scoring.pointsPerSecond}**. Add the two penalties.

## Why the eggs decide the flight

**One crack disqualifies the flight.** Cushion the eggs on all sides and separate them from each other and hard parts. A larger chute usually slows landing but increases flight time and drift. Test for both safe recovery and the target time.

## Before you build or fly

Work with an adult who has read the NAR safety code. Complete **Safety First** before the build and launch lessons. Students make the competition design; mentors teach and supervise.
