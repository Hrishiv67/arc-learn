## Air pushes back

**Drag** resists motion through air. It slows the rocket on the way up. The parachute uses that same resistance to slow the way down.

Try moving a flat hand through water, then turn it edge-on. The wide face is harder to move. A rocket's size, shape, surface, and speed also affect drag.

## What changes the height?

- **More drag:** usually less altitude with the same rocket and motor.
- **Extra mass:** changes acceleration, coasting, and stability. Its effect on peak height is not a simple always-up or always-down rule; simulate it.
- **Wind:** can turn a stable rocket into the wind, called _weathercocking_. A tilted flight usually reaches a lower vertical height.
- **Air conditions:** temperature and launch-site elevation change air density, so the same setup can fly differently on another day.

## Make a useful comparison

Compare flights with the same motor type and recorded weather. Check the real mass and dimensions before blaming drag. Rough joints, crooked fins, or a bent tube can explain a mismatch between the model and the flight.
